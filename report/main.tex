\begin{abstract}
We study coordinate-selection rules for ridge regression under feature
scaling and correlation. An initial frozen benchmark compares uniform
sampling, fixed Lipschitz sampling, periodically refreshed gradient-gain
sampling, and a progress-triggered gradient-gain heuristic across four
synthetic regimes and the packaged diabetes regression dataset.

We subsequently implement Approximate Steepest Coordinate Descent
(ASCD) of Stich, Raj, and Jaggi using the paper's zero oracle
$g^3$ with exact full-gradient initialization while retaining the
benchmark's exact ridge coordinate-minimization update. On the original
scaled-correlated case, this configuration reaches the target in 9 of
12 runs compared with 6 of 12 for uniform sampling, but records greater
proxy work on every seed where both methods succeed.

A Stage~4 oracle-quality analysis explains why zero-oracle ASCD provides
little pruning. Its certified lower bounds collapse to zero on almost
all coordinates, leaving an effectively full active set. Replacing the
zero oracle by the exact inner-product oracle $g^1$ reduces the median
active set to one coordinate. The tested simulated intermediate
approximate oracles do not provide a useful middle regime.

A predeclared Stage~5 confirmatory experiment then evaluates uniform,
fixed Lipschitz, $g^3$, and $g^1$ on four held-out Toeplitz synthetic
families and four additional real regression datasets, using a
dimension-normalized 200-coordinate-epoch budget, a primary normalized
objective-gap target of $10^{-6}$, harmonized timing boundaries, and
three single-threaded timing repetitions. The Stage~4 pruning mechanism
generalizes to all eight problem families: the median $g^3$ active-set
fraction is one and its median zero-lower-bound fraction is one, whereas
$g^1$ has a one-coordinate median active set. Exact information has
lower paired median time to target on five families but is substantially
slower on both scaled synthetic families; on the strongly scaled
correlated family, $g^1$ also reaches the primary target in only 9 of
12 runs compared with 12 of 12 for $g^3$. None of the four methods
reaches the primary target on Residential Building within the declared
budget. These results support a mechanism-level conclusion about oracle
quality and pruning, but not a claim of universal computational
superiority or algorithmic novelty.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and related work}
% =============================================================================

Randomized updates and adaptive information collection occur throughout
modern optimization, including stochastic block BFGS
\cite{gower2016}, minibatch semi-stochastic optimization
\cite{konecny2016}, parallel and accelerated coordinate descent
\cite{richtarik2016,fercoq2015}, and randomized iterative linear
solvers \cite{gower2015}. Adaptive coordinate-selection methods include
adaptive probabilities for stochastic dual coordinate ascent
\cite{csiba2015}, adaptive importance sampling
\cite{perekrestenko2017}, and Approximate Steepest Coordinate Descent
(ASCD) \cite{stich2017}.

These works prevent gradient-informed coordinate selection or refresh
rules alone from being treated as new ideas. The practical question in
this project is instead:

\begin{quote}
When does better information about coordinate quality improve
optimization enough to justify the cost of obtaining, maintaining,
and using it?
\end{quote}

The study is developed in four empirical layers.

First, a frozen baseline compares simple fixed and adaptive
coordinate-sampling rules. Second, the published ASCD selection
mechanism is implemented with the paper's zero oracle on the same
benchmark. Third, the oracle-quality mechanism is diagnosed by comparing
zero, approximate, and exact gradient oracles. Fourth, a predeclared
confirmatory experiment tests whether the resulting conclusions persist
on held-out synthetic structures and additional real regression
datasets under harmonized wall-clock accounting.

The later experiments are comparisons under this project's ridge
benchmark. They are not reproductions of the published ASCD paper's
figures.

\section{Experimental setup and baseline methods}
% =============================================================================

For a design matrix $X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}$ and response
$y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$, we minimize

\begin{equation}
f(w)
=
\frac{1}{2}\|Xw-y\|_2^2
+
\frac{\lambda}{2}\|w\|_2^2,
\qquad
\lambda=0.03.
\label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}

With residual

\[
r=Xw-y,
\]

the coordinate gradient and coordinate smoothness are

\begin{equation}
g_j=X_j^\top r+\lambda w_j,
\qquad
L_j=\|X_j\|_2^2+\lambda.
\label{eq:grad-lipschitz}
\end{equation}

Every method uses the same exact coordinate-minimization update,

\begin{equation}
w_j
\leftarrow
w_j-\frac{g_j}{L_j},
\label{eq:coordinate-update}
\end{equation}

with the residual updated incrementally.

Uniform sampling uses

\[
p_j=\frac{1}{d},
\]

whereas fixed Lipschitz sampling uses

\[
p_j
=
\frac{L_j}{\sum_k L_k}.
\]

The two baseline gradient-gain variants use

\begin{equation}
p_j
=
\frac{0.05}{d}
+
0.95
\frac{g_j^2/L_j}
{\sum_k g_k^2/L_k},
\label{eq:gain-probability}
\end{equation}

with uniform fallback when the denominator vanishes.

Scheduled gain refreshes initially and every 60 updates. Triggered gain
refreshes at step zero and subsequently at a 120-step checkpoint if the
latest objective decrease is below half the preceding decrease, subject
to a 60-step minimum interval.

The initial frozen benchmark contains four synthetic cases crossing
balanced versus geometrically scaled features with independent versus
eight-group correlated features. The synthetic cases use
$n=400$ and $d=48$. The packaged diabetes regression design provides
one additional real-data case.

Each baseline method uses 12 problem seeds, initializes at $w=0$, and
runs for at most 2,400 coordinate updates. A dense ridge solve defines
$f^\star$ for evaluation only. A baseline run succeeds if a saved
checkpoint satisfies

\[
f(w)-f^\star\le 10^{-4}.
\]

For the baseline study, the workload proxy counts modelled coordinate
update, sampling, refresh, and evaluation operations. These units are
bookkeeping quantities rather than measured floating-point-operation
counts.

\section{Published ASCD comparison}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Selection rule}

ASCD \cite{stich2017} maintains, for each coordinate $j$, a stored
gradient estimate $\widetilde g_j$ and certified radius $r_j$ satisfying

\begin{equation}
|g_j-\widetilde g_j|
\le r_j.
\label{eq:interval}
\end{equation}

The corresponding upper and lower magnitude bounds are

\begin{equation}
u_j
=
|\widetilde g_j|+r_j,
\qquad
\ell_j
=
\max\{0,|\widetilde g_j|-r_j\}.
\label{eq:upper-lower}
\end{equation}

The active set is the smallest nonempty set $I$ such that every
excluded coordinate satisfies

\begin{equation}
u_j^2
<
\frac{1}{|I|}
\sum_{i\in I}\ell_i^2.
\label{eq:active-set}
\end{equation}

The exclusion inequality is strict; equality therefore retains the
coordinate. Selection is uniform among active coordinates attaining the
largest lower bound.

Our Stage~3 implementation initializes $\widetilde g$ with the exact
full gradient and initializes all radii to zero.


\subsection{Zero oracle}

For Stage~3, the paper's zero cross-coordinate oracle $g^3$ is used.
If coordinate $i$ changes by $h$, then for $j\ne i$,

\begin{equation}
g_j^{\mathrm{new}}-g_j
=
hX_j^\top X_i.
\end{equation}

Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\begin{equation}
|g_j^{\mathrm{new}}-g_j|
\le
|h|\,\|X_i\|_2\|X_j\|_2.
\label{eq:zero-oracle-radius}
\end{equation}

The stored passive-coordinate estimate is left unchanged and its radius
is increased by this certified bound.

For the selected coordinate,

\[
h=-\frac{g_i}{L_i},
\]

so exact coordinate minimization gives

\[
g_i^{\mathrm{new}}
=
g_i+hL_i
=
0
\]

up to floating-point precision. Its stored estimate and radius can
therefore be reset to zero.


\subsection{Implementation verification}

The implementation includes a numerical audit on a small dense ridge
problem. After each audited update it recomputes

\[
X^\top(Xw-y)+\lambda w
\]

and verifies containment of every true coordinate gradient inside the
maintained interval. The residual invariant

\[
r=Xw-y
\]

is also checked.

The strict active-set rule is tested separately, including its equality
boundary, and the audited exact coordinate updates are required to be
nonincreasing in objective value.

\subsection{Stage 3 work accounting}

The Stage~3 ASCD accounting includes an initial full-gradient charge of

\[
nd+d.
\]

Each saved objective checkpoint adds $n+d$. Each coordinate update adds

\[
2n+3
+
d\left\lceil\log_2d\right\rceil
+
3d,
\]

representing the coordinate update, modelled active-set computation, and
interval maintenance.

These are approximate bookkeeping units rather than exact FLOP counts.
Stage~3 baseline and ASCD timer boundaries were not fully harmonized, so
the recorded wall times from that stage are not used as controlled
method comparisons.

\section{Stage 4: oracle-quality and active-set mechanism analysis}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Why does the zero-oracle active set remain full?}

Stage~4 first replays the frozen Stage~3 zero-oracle trajectory while
recording per-update active-set and interval diagnostics. The replay
reproduces the frozen Stage~3 objective checkpoints and proxy work.

For the four 48-coordinate synthetic cases, the full active set is used
on approximately $99.94\%$ of updates. On diabetes, the full
10-coordinate active set is used on approximately $99.96\%$ of updates.

The median fraction of coordinates with zero certified lower bound is
one on every case.

Since

\[
\ell_j
=
\max\{0,|\widetilde g_j|-r_j\},
\]

a lower bound becomes zero whenever the certified radius is at least as
large as the stored estimate magnitude. If all lower bounds are zero,

\[
\frac{1}{|I|}
\sum_{i\in I}\ell_i^2
=
0.
\]

The strict exclusion condition in
Equation~\eqref{eq:active-set} would then require

\[
u_j^2<0,
\]

which is impossible. The observed radius trajectories are consistent
with this mechanism: zero-oracle uncertainty typically dominates the
stored gradient information.


\subsection{Oracle-quality experiment}

Phase~B retains the same frozen benchmark and exact coordinate update
while varying the gradient oracle.

The configurations are

\[
g^3,
\qquad
g^2(\epsilon),
\quad
\epsilon\in
\{1,0.5,0.25,0.125\},
\qquad
g^1.
\]

Here $g^1$ is the exact inner-product oracle. The simulated $g^2$
configurations use certified error

\[
\delta_{ij}
=
\epsilon\|X_i\|_2\|X_j\|_2.
\]

The simulated $g^2$ experiment isolates oracle-quality effects. It does

\subsection{Active-set transition}

The exact oracle produces a qualitatively different active-set regime.

For $g^1$, the median active set is one coordinate on every Stage~4
case, and the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th active-set percentiles are
all one. By contrast, $g^3$ and $g^2(1)$ retain an effectively full
active set.

The intermediate simulated $g^2$ configurations do prune frequently,
but only weakly: the median synthetic active set is 47 of 48 coordinates
and the diabetes median is 9 of 10 coordinates.

Thus the tested precision grid does not provide a useful intermediate
regime between zero-oracle uncertainty and exact information.

\subsection{Optimization behavior}

The objective-gap trajectories show a similarly sharp separation.
Exact $g^1$ reaches the Stage~4 target on every run. The
$\epsilon=1$ simulated configuration largely resembles the zero oracle.

The tested $\epsilon\in\{0.5,0.25,0.125\}$ configurations do not reach
the target on any Stage~4 run.

This nonmonotonic behavior should be interpreted narrowly. Changing the
approximate oracle changes both the maintained estimates and the
resulting coordinate-selection path. The result does not establish that
a more accurate approximate oracle is generally harmful; rather, the
tested simulated settings fail to produce a useful middle regime on
this frozen benchmark.

\section{Stage 5: confirmatory robustness and runtime validation}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Predeclared held-out protocol}

Stage~5 tests whether the Stage~4 mechanism persists outside the
development benchmark.

The primary confirmatory question is:

\begin{quote}
Do the Stage~4 conclusions about oracle uncertainty, active-set pruning,
and the cost of exact information persist on held-out synthetic
structures and additional real regression datasets under harmonized
runtime accounting?
\end{quote}

Only four primary methods are retained:

\begin{itemize}
    \item uniform coordinate sampling;
    \item fixed Lipschitz sampling;
    \item zero-oracle ASCD $g^3$;
    \item exact-inner-product ASCD $g^1$.
\end{itemize}

The unsuccessful simulated Stage~4 $g^2$ precision grid is not included
in the primary confirmatory study.

The held-out synthetic generator is deliberately different from the
development generator. It uses

\[
n=800,
\qquad
d=96,
\]

with Toeplitz covariance

\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{ij}
=
\rho^{|i-j|},
\qquad
\rho\in\{0.6,0.9\}.
\label{eq:toeplitz}
\end{equation}

Balanced and geometrically scaled versions of each correlation level
produce four held-out synthetic families. Scaled designs use

\[
\operatorname{logspace}(-1.5,1.5,d).
\]

Twelve problem seeds, 100 through 111, are used.

Four additional real regression datasets are included:
California Housing, Concrete Compressive Strength, Airfoil Self-Noise,
and Residential Building. After the fixed 80/20 split and training-only
standardization, the corresponding training dimensions are

\[
16512\times 8,\quad
824\times 8,\quad
1202\times 5,\quad
297\times 107.
\]

For Residential Building, the sale-price output $V$-9 is the response;
the second output $V$-10 is excluded from the design. Repeated economic
variables are identified by their five source time lags.

Because Stage~5 spans substantially different sample sizes and scales,
the primary convergence measure is the normalized objective gap

\begin{equation}
R_t
=
\frac{f(w_t)-f^\star}
{\max\{f(w_0)-f^\star,10^{-30}\}}.
\label{eq:relative-gap}
\end{equation}

The primary target is

\[
R_t\le10^{-6},
\]

and the secondary target is

\[
R_t\le10^{-4}.
\]

The maximum optimization budget is

\[
200d
\]

coordinate updates, i.e.\ 200 coordinate epochs, with checkpoints every
$5d$ updates.

For timing, each method/problem pair receives one untimed warm-up and
three timed repetitions. The timing boundary begins before
method-specific setup and includes coordinate selection, updates,
internal ASCD maintenance, and the exact Gram acquisition for $g^1$.
Checkpoint evaluation and the dense evaluation optimum are excluded from
the primary method timer. Numerical kernels are constrained to one BLAS
thread.

one of the eight Stage~5 problem families. The median fraction of
coordinates with zero certified lower bound is likewise 1.0 on all
eight.

on every problem family. Consequently its median active-set fraction is

\[
\frac{1}{96}\approx0.0104
\]

on the held-out synthetic families,

\[
\frac18=0.125
\]

on California and Concrete,

\[
\frac15=0.2
\]

on Airfoil, and

\[
\frac1{107}\approx0.00935
\]

on Residential Building.

The Stage~4 explanation therefore survives the held-out experiment:
zero-oracle uncertainty repeatedly collapses the lower bounds and leaves
the safe active set effectively unpruned, whereas exact oracle
information produces one-coordinate selection.

\subsection{Real-data predictive diagnostics}

Stage~5 remains an optimization experiment rather than a predictive
machine-learning model-selection study.

All four methods optimize the same training ridge objective. Test RMSE
is therefore recorded only as a secondary diagnostic of the final
iterates.

On California, Concrete, and Airfoil, the methods that converge tightly
to the ridge optimum also produce test RMSE values numerically very close
to the dense reference solution.

Residential Building illustrates why these RMSE values should not be
used to rank the optimization methods. None of the methods reaches the
primary optimization target, and some incompletely optimized iterates
obtain lower test RMSE than the exact training optimum. For example, the
median final $g^3$ test RMSE is below the dense ridge reference test
RMSE even though $g^3$ has a substantially larger training objective
gap. This is possible because minimizing the fixed training objective is
not equivalent to minimizing test RMSE.

Accordingly, Stage~5 does not interpret test RMSE differences as an
optimization-method superiority result.


\subsection{Predeclared confirmatory decisions}

The Stage~5 protocol specified four mechanism and cost questions.

The resulting decisions are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item
    \textbf{Zero-oracle pruning failure.}
    The median $g^3$ active-set fraction exceeds the $g^1$ fraction on
    all eight problem families, exceeding the predeclared majority
    requirement.

    \item
    \textbf{Exact-information pruning.}
    Exact $g^1$ reduces the median active set to one coordinate on every
    family. It also uses fewer paired median coordinate epochs than
    $g^3$ on the two balanced synthetic families and on California,
    Concrete, and Airfoil.

    \item
    \textbf{Regime-dependent information cost.}
    The paired median runtime ratio is below one on five families and
    above one on both scaled synthetic families. Residential has no
    shared primary success and therefore no time-to-target ratio.

    \item
    \textbf{Generalization of the Stage~4 mechanism.}
    Both the active-set and zero-lower-bound criteria hold on all eight
    held-out families.
\end{enumerate}

The predeclared rule that exact information can sometimes pay for itself
is therefore satisfied. The confirmatory evidence does not support a
universal exact-oracle advantage.

\section{Interpretation and limitations}
% =============================================================================

The combined experiments support a narrower and more defensible
conclusion than a general claim that adaptive coordinate selection is
better.

The baseline experiment demonstrates that simple gradient-gain refresh
heuristics are highly sensitive to problem structure and do not
consistently improve on fixed sampling.

Stage~3 shows that a published ASCD selection mechanism can improve
success count on one difficult development regime, but that the zero
oracle retains a full active set and can incur greater recorded work.

Stage~4 identifies the mechanism behind that behavior. The
Cauchy--Schwarz zero-oracle radii are sufficiently broad that certified
lower bounds collapse to zero on almost all coordinates. The safe
exclusion test then has essentially no opportunity to prune. Exact
inner-product information removes that uncertainty and produces a
one-coordinate active set.

Stage~5 provides the strongest evidence for this mechanism because the
same qualitative effect appears on all four held-out Toeplitz synthetic
families and all four additional real datasets. The mechanism is
therefore not confined to the original grouped-correlation synthetic
generator or the diabetes dataset.

However, aggressive pruning is not equivalent to universal computational
advantage. On two balanced synthetic families and three real datasets,
the reduction in coordinate effort is large enough that $g^1$ has a
lower measured time to the primary target than $g^3$. On both scaled
synthetic families the direction reverses. In the strongest scaled case,
$g^1$ also has a lower primary success count.

Several limitations remain.

First, the experiments concern convex ridge regression with exact
coordinate minimization. They do not establish the same behavior for
general smooth objectives, nonconvex machine-learning models, or
high-dimensional sparse learning problems.

Second, the Stage~5 real datasets broaden the evaluation substantially
relative to the original diabetes-only benchmark, but four additional
regression datasets are still a limited empirical sample.

Third, measured runtimes remain implementation- and hardware-dependent.
Stage~5 improves internal validity through harmonized timing,
single-threaded numerical kernels, warm-up execution, and repeated
measurement, but its absolute timing ratios should not be assumed to
transfer unchanged to other implementations.

Fourth, proxy work remains a modelled accounting quantity rather than
an exact floating-point-operation count.

Fifth, the exact oracle obtains its information through a dense Gram
matrix. This is a useful controlled reference point but may be
impractical for genuinely large $d$. The tested Stage~4 simulated
approximate oracles do not establish a practical approximate alternative.

Sixth, the theoretical one-step comparison in Stich et al. should not be
treated as a direct guarantee for this benchmark. This project retains
coordinate-specific $L_j$ values and exact coordinate minimization, so
its experimental update convention differs from assumptions required
for a direct application of the paper's comparison theorem.

Finally, objective gaps reported at or near zero can be limited by
floating-point precision. All convergence claims should therefore be
understood relative to the declared numerical targets and implementation
precision.


% =============================================================================
\section{Conclusion and future research}
% =============================================================================

This study began with a simple practical question: when does the
additional information used by adaptive coordinate selection justify
its computational cost?

The baseline study shows that the answer cannot be reduced to
``adaptive is better.'' The tested gradient-gain refresh heuristics
succeed on some regimes and fail on others.

The published zero-oracle ASCD comparison likewise gives a mixed result.
It increases scaled-correlated success count on the original benchmark
but provides almost no active-set pruning and records greater paired
proxy work than uniform sampling on their shared successful instances.

The Stage~4 oracle analysis identifies a concrete mechanism for this
failure to prune. Under $g^3$, certified uncertainty drives the lower
bounds to zero and leaves the safe active set effectively full. Exact
$g^1$ reverses this behavior and reduces selection to one coordinate.

Most importantly, Stage~5 confirms that this mechanism persists beyond
the development benchmark. On all eight held-out problem families,
zero-oracle ASCD retains a full median active set with complete median
lower-bound collapse, whereas exact-oracle ASCD has a one-coordinate
median active set.

The computational consequence is regime-dependent. Exact information
has lower paired median time to target on five Stage~5 families but is
more than three times as slow as $g^3$ on the two scaled synthetic
families under the measured implementation. On the strongest scaled
family it also succeeds less often at the primary target. Residential
Building supplies a separate negative result in which no method reaches
the primary target within the declared budget.

The resulting research conclusion is therefore not that exact
information is always worth acquiring. Rather, \emph{oracle information
quality strongly determines whether the ASCD active-set mechanism can
prune, but whether that information pays for itself depends on the
problem regime and information-acquisition cost}.

A publication-oriented extension could investigate a genuinely costed
approximate inner-product oracle or a theoretically motivated
information-acquisition rule. Such an extension should define the
acquisition mechanism and its cost before inspecting results and should
be evaluated separately from the completed confirmatory study.

Before any such methodological extension, the present work can be
completed through a final reproducibility and synthesis stage:
auditing all reported numbers against the generated artifacts, freezing
the experimental outputs, pinning the computational environment,
documenting exact reproduction commands, and performing a final
page-by-page report review.
